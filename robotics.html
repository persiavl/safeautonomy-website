The problem

A robot can be safe in the lab and unsafe everywhere else.

Functional safety, AI trustworthiness and certifiability are three different failure modes. A platform can clear one and quietly fail the other two — and the gap only shows up once it's deployed.

The robot fails

A motor controller drifts out of calibration, a bus message is corrupted, a watchdog misses its deadline. Classic malfunctioning behaviour of an E/E/PE safety-related system — random hardware faults and systematic faults in hardware and software.

Covered by IEC 61508

The AI can't be trusted

Every actuator is healthy and every joint is within tolerance, and the arm still grips the wrong part or the AMR still misreads the aisle — because the model's confidence was never calibrated to the situation it met. Insufficiency in perception or decision-making, not a fault.

Covered by TR 5469 · TS 22440 · TS 25223

Nobody can prove it's safe enough

The robot works in the lab and in every demo you've run. A customer, insurer or certification body needs an argument, not a demo reel — evidence of what was tested, what wasn't, and why the residual risk is acceptable.

Covered by UL 4600 · UL 3115

The seam is the risk. A mobile platform can pass every IEC 61508 safety function test and still misclassify a person as a pallet. It can have a well-behaved perception model and still have no assurance case a customer's insurer will accept. Only an argument that spans all three can close the case.

We bridge six standards into one safety argument

One hazard. Three families of cause. One safety case that traces every hazard through every applicable framework to evidence — whatever the robot is built to do.

Who we serve

Four kinds of machine, one safety discipline

The physical form changes what can go wrong. It doesn't change the questions we ask about it.

AGVs & AMRs

Automated guided vehicles and autonomous mobile robots navigating warehouses, factories and hospitals — where ISO 3691-4 and ANSI/RIA R15.08 meet functional safety and AI perception.

Moving platforms

Wheeled and tracked platforms carrying payloads or people, where safety-rated stopping, obstacle detection and autonomy assurance have to work together.

Robot arms

Fixed and mobile manipulators performing tasks alongside people or fully autonomously, where safety functions and AI-driven grasping both need to be proven safe.

Humanoids

Bipedal and human-scale robots combining actuation, perception and decision-making in one platform — the hardest single case for an integrated safety argument.

Regulatory readiness

The standards are the means. Certification is the end.

Standards conformance is necessary but not sufficient. We shape the technical evidence so it maps directly onto the regulatory instrument or certificate you actually need.

EU Machinery Reg. 2023/1230

The new EU rulebook for machinery, applicable from January 2027, with expanded scope that now explicitly covers autonomous mobile machinery, AI-based safety systems, industrial robots and cobots.

ISO 3691-4

The core Type-C safety standard for driverless industrial trucks and their systems — the reference point for AGV and AMR safety functions, person detection and braking performance.

ANSI/RIA R15.08

Industrial mobile robot safety requirements for the US market, covering the robot itself, system integration and — in its forthcoming Part 3 — the user's operating environment.

UL certification pathway

Where standards conformance turns into a certificate: UL 4600 safety-case evaluation and UL 3115 AI-product certification, mapped against ISO, IEC and NIST frameworks for cross-recognition.
